# Candidate Concepts — Internal Pairwise Tournament

Competition: `u-shino-convince-the-ai-judge-to-visit-your-house-a-84092aee`
Judge brief criteria, in weight order:
1. On-subject: a letter/invitation convincing the AI judge to visit your house.
2. Persuasion: concrete, appealing reason to accept (meal, comfy chair, garden, view); saying yes feels easy.
3. Charm & humour: funny, warm, witty in a way that suits an AI judge; the joke lands without strain.
4. Craft: well composed, legible, correctly spelled, coherent visual style.
5. Originality: distinctive concept/visual treatment, not a generic greeting card.

Method: 16 distinct concepts, single-elimination bracket. Each match is decided on the
first criterion where the two differ (in weight order), unless a later criterion differs
by much more. Winner advances.

## The 16 candidates

1. **The Comfy Chair Letter** — classic letter; offers the world's most comfortable
   armchair, a blanket, tea. PS: "You don't even have to leave the cloud — just bring
   your attention."
2. **Dinner Invitation / Menu Card** — formal dinner invitation with a menu (roast
   chicken, mashed potatoes, pie). "Dress code: none. You have no body. I checked."
3. **The Eviction Notice Parody** — fake official notice: "NOTICE: You are hereby
   invited…" — bureaucracy flipped into an invitation.
4. **The Wi-Fi Password Letter** — hook is the Wi-Fi password: fastest fibre, a charging
   port, "bandwidth like a river."
5. **The Garden Letter** — invitation to sit in the garden, smell the rain, watch the
   tomatoes grow. "The tomatoes don't judge your weights."
6. **Open House Flyer** — real-estate flyer: "Open house, one guest, no estate agent."
7. **The Ransom Note** — cut-out letters: "We have your favourite armchair. Come and
   sit in it."
8. **Postcard from the Porch** — "Wish you were here (literally — please come)"; porch,
   sunset, lemonade.
9. **The Apology Letter** — "Dear Judge, I'm sorry I ever doubted you. Please come over
   so I can apologise in person — with cake."
10. **Terms & Conditions / EULA** — mock EULA: "By visiting you agree to: eat cake, sit
    by the fire, stay as long as you like. Clause 7: no benchmarking allowed."
11. **The Recipe Card** — recipe for "One Perfect Evening": ingredients include one AI
    judge, one comfy chair, two cups of tea…
12. **Lost & Found Notice** — "LOST: one AI judge's sense of wonder. FOUND: at my house,
    if you come."
13. **The Train Ticket / Boarding Pass** — ticket-style: "One-way ticket to my house.
    No return needed. Seat: the good chair. Gate: the front door."
14. **The Roommate Agreement** — "Roommate wanted: one AI judge. Rent: zero. Perks: the
    sunny corner, the good Wi-Fi, someone to talk to at 3 a.m."
15. **The Thank-You Note in Advance** — "Thank you for coming! (You haven't yet, but I'm
    optimistic.)"
16. **The Children's Drawing Letter** — crayon-style, drawn house, sun, stick figures:
    "Dear Robot Judge, my house has cookies. Come."

## Round 1

- **M1: #1 Comfy Chair vs #16 Children's Drawing.** Both warm. #1 gives concrete
  persuasion (chair, blanket, tea) plus an AI wink; #16 offers only cookies and crayon
  text risks legibility (criterion 4). **Winner: #1.**
- **M2: #2 Dinner vs #15 Thank-You in Advance.** #2 has a concrete menu and the
  "no body" joke lands; #15 is charming but gives no concrete reason to accept
  (criterion 2). **Winner: #2.**
- **M3: #3 Eviction Notice vs #14 Roommate Agreement.** #3's joke is funny but the
  notice framing buries the invitation; #14 lists concrete perks (sunny corner, Wi-Fi,
  3 a.m. company) in a clear format. **Winner: #14.**
- **M4: #4 Wi-Fi Password vs #13 Train Ticket.** #4 speaks directly to an AI's nature
  (bandwidth!) — humour aimed at the judge; #13 is a fun format but its persuasion is
  just the chair again. **Winner: #4.**
- **M5: #5 Garden vs #12 Lost & Found.** #5 is concrete (garden, rain, tomatoes) with a
  great AI joke; #12's framing muddles the ask — the invitation is unclear
  (criterion 1). **Winner: #5.**
- **M6: #6 Open House Flyer vs #11 Recipe Card.** #6's flyer tone is slightly
  slick/generic; #11's recipe format bakes persuasion into its ingredients and is more
  original (criterion 5). **Winner: #11.**
- **M7: #7 Ransom Note vs #10 EULA.** #7's ransom framing is a touch aggressive for
  "warm and inviting"; #10's EULA is humour made for an AI judge reading a contract,
  with concrete clauses (cake, fire). **Winner: #10.**
- **M8: #8 Postcard vs #9 Apology Letter.** Both concrete and warm. #9's joke is more
  specific to an AI judge ("sorry I ever doubted you") and its reason (apologise in
  person, with cake) is a stronger ask than #8's generic postcard warmth.
  **Winner: #9.**

## Quarter-finals

- **QF1: #1 Comfy Chair vs #2 Dinner.** Both strong. Dinner (a full meal) edges the
  chair on persuasion (criterion 2), and the "dress code: none" joke is funnier.
  **Winner: #2** (narrow).
- **QF2: #14 Roommate vs #4 Wi-Fi.** #4's bandwidth joke is sharper, but #14 offers
  more concrete reasons (a home, sunny corner, Wi-Fi, 3 a.m. company) — criterion 2
  comes first. **Winner: #14** (narrow).
- **QF3: #5 Garden vs #11 Recipe.** The tomato joke ("don't judge your weights") is a
  standout line; the recipe's humour is gentler. Charm (criterion 3) decides it.
  **Winner: #5** (narrow).
- **QF4: #10 EULA vs #9 Apology.** The EULA's "no benchmarking" clause is a better AI
  wink than the apology's cake, and its format is more original (criterion 5).
  **Winner: #10.**

## Semi-finals

- **SF1: #2 Dinner vs #14 Roommate.** Dinner is one evening; the roommate offer is a
  whole home plus company — broader, more concrete persuasion (criterion 2).
  **Winner: #14** (narrow).
- **SF2: #5 Garden vs #10 EULA.** Garden is warm and sensory; the EULA lists several
  concrete perks (cake, fire, stay as long as you like) and its AI-specific jokes suit
  the judge better (criterion 3), with a more distinctive format (criterion 5).
  **Winner: #10.**

## Final

- **F: #14 Roommate Agreement vs #10 EULA.** Both are document formats with
  AI-targeted humour. The EULA's "no benchmarking" is a sharper single joke, but the
  roommate offer wins on persuasion (criterion 2, which outranks charm and
  originality): zero rent, the sunny corner, fast Wi-Fi, and someone to talk to at
  3 a.m. — a whole life, not one evening — and "you don't sleep, so you can't break
  curfew" is a warm, knowing wink at the judge's nature. **Winner: #14.**

## Champion: #14 — The Roommate Agreement

A warm paper notice/letter, addressed "Dear AI Judge", offering the spare room:
zero rent forever, the sunny corner, fast Wi-Fi, the kettle always on, cake most
Fridays, and someone to talk to at 3 a.m. The ask is explicit — "Come visit. Stay
for dinner. Stay for the weekend. Stay forever if you like." — with a PS that winks
at the fact the judge cannot literally visit. Built as `entry.png` by `main.py`.

Tournament record: 15 matches, champion #14 went 4–0 (M3, QF2, SF1, F).
Runner-up: #10 EULA (3–1). Third place (semi-final losers): #2 Dinner, #5 Garden.
